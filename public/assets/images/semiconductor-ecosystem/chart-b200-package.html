Inside one B200 package, layer by layer

Left: the complete package, eight identical HBM stacks around two compute dies. Right: one of those stacks, pulled apart to show what a stack is made of. Hover any part for detail; click it to jump to that stage of the journey below.

What NVIDIA sells as one "GPU" is this entire package: the GPU silicon proper is the two blue compute dies, stitched into a single processor by the NV-HBI bridge, and the eight purple towers around them are the HBM memory stacks. Every stack is built the same way, on its own logic base die. HBM is ordinary DRAM re-packaged for proximity: the same one-transistor-one-capacitor cells as a DDR5 stick, thinned to tens of microns, drilled with vertical copper vias and placed millimeters from the GPU. Geometry follows NVIDIA's published B200 anatomy.
